Chapter Twelve

The Signal Between Stars

Kesia found the second message the way she found most things that mattered: by accident, at 3 a.m., when the station's systems ran quiet enough to hear themselves think. It sat in the maintenance log like a splinter — eleven seconds of telemetry from a Meridian that hadn't launched yet, timestamped four years in the future.

She read it twice before she let herself believe it. Then a third time, because belief was not the same as understanding, and understanding was what she needed if she was going to keep this from Director Ashworth for even one more day.

Don't trust the orbit, the message said, in her own handwriting. It isn't yours anymore.

Outside the viewport, Earth turned its slow blue shoulder toward the dark, indifferent to the fact that somewhere above it, a version of Kesia who had already made her choice was trying to warn the version who hadn't. She pressed her palm to the glass, half expecting to feel the future on the other side of it — some faint warmth, some proof. There was only cold.

Ninety days, the station's countdown read. Ninety days until Meridian's next long-burn maneuver, the one that would carry them past the point where a message like this could still arrive in time to matter.

She had ninety days to decide whether the woman writing to her from the future was a warning or a threat — and whether there was still a difference.
